# User interview — Natalie (2026-09-09)

Answers gathered in the evaluator session before research reports landed.

## Framing decisions
- Audience: **me first, others later** — design for a persona (overwhelmed student/worker), keep it sharable.
- Phone: **both / not sure** — design for iPhone's stricter web-push rules; Android follows.
- Sync: **yes, real cross-device sync** on a free tier.
- Repo name: Claude proposes 3; folder created after she picks.
- "Claude design" = the Claude Design canvas skill, used at the Design Direction stage for mockups.
- Priority: secondary to the job search (stated and acknowledged).

## Habits
- Tasks currently live in **Notion / Obsidian / text files** — free-form, no reminders.
- Why she quits productivity apps (all four apply):
  1. Reminders felt like guilt.
  2. Too much setup / upkeep.
  3. Missed once, then gave up.
  4. The list got too long → overwhelm → closed it.
- Nudge timing: **let the app learn from when she responds**, with quiet hours she sets.
- Encouragement that works (all three):
  1. Progress she can see (bars, done-counts, "3 of 5 chunks").
  2. A companion / character that reacts.
  3. Small rewards / unlocks (a teaser, a fact, an earned break).
- Explicitly NOT chosen: "calm plain words, no mascot" — she wants a character.

## Implications for the proposal
- Zero-setup capture must be the default; structure is optional and added by the app, not the user.
- No streak-break punishment; lapse-return flow must be guilt-free.
- The visible list must be short (today's picks), never the full backlog.
- Adaptive reminder timing is a core feature, not a nice-to-have.
- Companion + visible progress + earned micro-rewards is the engagement loop to design.
